# TrAC Trends in Analytical Chemistry — Review Article Format & Writing Guide

> 目标期刊：TrAC - Trends in Analytical Chemistry (Elsevier, Q1, IF~12-14)

## 1. Journal Profile

| Item | Specification |
|---|---|
| **Full name** | Trends in Analytical Chemistry |
| **Abbreviation** | TrAC Trends Anal. Chem. |
| **Publisher** | Elsevier |
| **ISSN** | 0165-9936 |
| **Frequency** | Monthly |
| **Scope** | Concise, critical overviews of new developments in analytical chemistry |
| **Article type** | **Review only** — primary research papers are NOT within scope |
| **Invitation** | Most articles are by invitation of Contributing Editors |
| **Language** | English |

**Target readership**: analytical chemists and other users of analytical techniques — not just specialists. Writing must be accessible to non-specialists.

---

## 2. Hard Quantitative Limits (Non-negotiable)

| Parameter | Requirement | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| **Word count** | ~4,000 words (body text) | Concise critical overview, not exhaustive survey |
| **Figures + Tables** | ~4 total | Each figure must carry unique information; no redundancy |
| **References** | ~50 maximum | Must include other reviews + recent articles; prioritize quality over quantity |
| **Abstract** | Typically 150–250 words | Structured or unstructured; state scope + key findings |
| **Graphical Abstract** | Required (Elsevier) | Single visual summarizing the review's core message |
| **Highlights** | Required (Elsevier) | 3–5 bullet points, max 85 characters each |

> **Critical principle**: TrAC is a **trends** journal, not a **comprehensive** review journal. The 4000-word / 50-reference limit forces critical selection. Every cited paper must serve an argument; every figure must illustrate a trend or mechanism.

---

## 3. Standard Manuscript Structure

```
Title
  ↓
Authors + Affiliations
  ↓
Abstract (150–250 words)
  ↓
Keywords (4–6)
  ↓
Graphical Abstract (single figure)
  ↓
Highlights (3–5 bullets, ≤85 chars each)
  ↓
1. Introduction
     — State objectives of the review
     — Provide adequate background
     — AVOID detailed literature survey or results summary
  ↓
2. [Main Body — Discussion]
     — Organized into numbered subsections by theme/technology/application
     — Each subsection: trend identification → evidence → critical evaluation
     — ~3–5 subsections typical for 4000 words
  ↓
3. Conclusions / Summary and Outlook
     — Short (1–2 paragraphs)
     — Key conclusions + future challenges + emerging directions
  ↓
Acknowledgements (optional)
  ↓
References (~50)
```

## 4. Section-by-Section Writing Techniques

### 4.1 Title

- **Pattern**: "[Topic]: [angle/scope]" or "[Trend] in [field]"
- **Examples from TrAC**:
  - *"Recent trends in two-dimensional liquid chromatography"*
  - *"Flow-based automated systems coupled with luminescence detection for non-metal determination: A comprehensive review from 2000 till present"*
- **Rules**: Include the key technique/analyte/application; avoid vague titles; if time-bounded, state the period.

### 4.2 Abstract

**Structure (4 moves)**:
1. **Context** (1 sentence): Why this topic matters now
2. **Scope** (1 sentence): What this review covers (time range, sub-topics)
3. **Key findings/trends** (2–3 sentences): The main patterns identified
4. **Outlook** (1 sentence): Future directions or gaps

**Example abstract structure** (from van den Hurk et al., 2023, TrAC 166, 117166):
> *"Multi-dimensional liquid chromatography (MD-LC) continues to gain in popularity for applications where conventional one-dimensional liquid chromatography is insufficient to solve the analytical problem at hand. **[Context]** In this review we have focused on articles published in the years 2019 to early 2023 and look for trends using our previous review published in 2018 as a baseline. **[Scope + baseline]** We have also explored usage patterns related to involvement of industrial laboratories... **[Key findings]***"

### 4.3 Introduction

**TrAC-specific rules** (from Guide for Authors):
- State the **objectives** of the review explicitly
- Provide **adequate background** for non-specialists
- **AVOID** a detailed literature survey (that belongs in the body)
- **AVOID** a summary of results (that belongs in conclusions)

**Recommended structure (3 paragraphs)**:
1. **Broader context**: Why analytical chemistry needs this topic; real-world driver (regulation, technology breakthrough, emerging contaminant)
2. **Knowledge gap**: What existing reviews cover; what is missing or outdated; why a new review is timely
3. **Scope & roadmap**: Explicitly state what this review covers (and what it does not); list the main sections; state the time range of literature surveyed

### 4.4 Main Body (Discussion)

This is the core of a TrAC review. **Organize by theme, not by paper.**

**Three common organizational patterns**:

| Pattern | When to use | Example structure |
|---|---|---|
| **By technology** | Multiple methods exist | 2.1 Method A; 2.2 Method B; 2.3 Method C |
| **By application** | Technique applied to many fields | 2.1 Environmental; 2.2 Biomedical; 2.3 Food |
| **By mechanism** | Fundamental understanding is key | 2.1 Molecular basis; 2.2 Additive effects; 2.3 Environmental modification |

**Writing technique per subsection — the "Trend-Evidence-Critique" (TEC) model**:

```
2.X [Subsection Title]

  [Trend statement] — 1 sentence asserting the key trend or pattern
      ↓
  [Evidence] — 2–4 sentences citing representative papers (not all papers)
      — Use comparative language: "while X found..., Y demonstrated..."
      — Quantify when possible: "over 60% of studies since 2020 use..."
      ↓
  [Critical evaluation] — 1–2 sentences assessing strengths/limitations/gaps
      — "However, most studies suffer from..."
      — "A major unresolved question is..."
```

**Key writing principles**:
- **Cite selectively**: With ~50 references for 4000 words, average ~12.5 references per 1000 words. Cite the most representative, not all.
- **Use comparative language**: "in contrast to", "whereas", "unlike", "consistent with", "building on"
- **Quantify trends**: "Since 2019, the number of studies using X has tripled"
- **Identify gaps explicitly**: "No study has yet addressed..." / "A critical knowledge gap remains..."
- **Avoid "laundry list" structure**: Do not write "Smith et al. did X. Jones et al. did Y. Wang et al. did Z." Instead, synthesize: "Three approaches have emerged: X (Smith, 2020), Y (Jones, 2021), and Z (Wang, 2022), each with distinct trade-offs..."

### 4.5 Conclusions / Summary and Outlook

**TrAC convention**: Short, forward-looking. Not a repeat of the abstract.

**Structure (2 paragraphs)**:
1. **Summary of key conclusions** (1 paragraph): 3–4 bullet-like sentences summarizing the main trends identified. Do not introduce new literature.
2. **Outlook / Future directions** (1 paragraph): 2–3 specific, actionable future research directions. Identify the most pressing unresolved questions. Mention emerging technologies or methodologies that could advance the field.

**Avoid**: Vague statements like "more research is needed." Be specific: "Future studies should systematically compare X across Y conditions using standardized Z protocols."

## 5. Figure & Table Requirements

### 5.1 Quantitative specs

| Item | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **Total figures + tables** | ~4 (can be 3–5) |
| **Figure resolution** | ≥300 dpi for color/halftone; ≥600 dpi for line art |
| **Figure format** | TIFF, EPS, or PDF (vector preferred); JPEG only for photos |
| **Color** | Color figures free of charge (Elsevier) |
| **Figure width** | Single column: ~85 mm; Double column: ~170 mm |
| **Font in figures** | Arial/Helvetica, 6–8 pt minimum after reduction |
| **Panel labels** | (a), (b), (c) — bold, consistent position |

### 5.2 Typical figure composition for a TrAC review

For a 4-figure review, the standard pattern is:

| Figure | Type | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **Figure 1** | Conceptual / mechanism diagram | Illustrate the fundamental principle or framework of the topic |
| **Figure 2** | Comparative / data plot | Compare methods, show trends across time or conditions |
| **Figure 3** | Application / workflow diagram | Show how the technique is applied in practice |
| **Figure 4** | Outlook / future directions schematic | Summarize challenges and future pathways |

**Table** (if used): Typically a comparison table of key methods/studies with columns: Method, Analyte, LOD, Sample matrix, Key advantage, Limitation, Reference.

### 5.3 Graphical Abstract (separate from main figures)

- Single figure, no panels
- Must visually communicate the review's core message
- Typical size: 5 × 3 inches (landscape)
- Can reuse/simplify Figure 1, but must be self-contained

## 6. Reference Style (Elsevier Vancouver)

- **Format**: Numbered in order of appearance, bracketed `[1]`
- **Journal abbreviation**: TrAC Trends Anal. Chem. (use standard abbreviations)
- **Example**:
  > [1] R.S. van den Hurk, M. Pursch, D.R. Stoll, B.W.J. Pirok, Recent trends in two-dimensional liquid chromatography, TrAC Trends Anal. Chem. 166 (2023) 117166.

- **Key rule**: ~50 references maximum. Prioritize: (1) recent high-impact papers, (2) foundational papers, (3) other reviews that readers can follow.

## 7. Case Study Analysis

### Case 1: "Recent trends in two-dimensional liquid chromatography"
- **Citation**: van den Hurk et al., TrAC 166 (2023) 117166
- **DOI**: 10.1016/j.trac.2023.117166
- **Authors**: 4 authors (academic + industrial collaboration)
- **Technique used**: **Baseline comparison** — explicitly uses their 2018 review as a baseline to identify new trends in 2019–2023
- **Scope statement**: "focused on articles published in the years 2019 to early 2023"
- **Additional angle**: Explores "usage patterns related to involvement of industrial laboratories" — adds a sociological/quantitative dimension beyond technique review
- **Writing lesson**: A TrAC review should have a **clear analytical angle**, not just a literature summary. The baseline-comparison approach is highly effective for trend identification.

## 8. Submission Checklist

### Pre-submission
- [ ] Word count ≤ 4,500 (target ~4,000)
- [ ] Total figures + tables ≤ 5 (target ~4)
- [ ] References ≤ 55 (target ~50)
- [ ] Abstract 150–250 words
- [ ] Graphical abstract prepared (separate file)
- [ ] Highlights: 3–5 bullets, each ≤85 characters
- [ ] Keywords: 4–6
- [ ] All figures ≥300 dpi, labeled (a)(b)(c), fonts ≥6pt
- [ ] Introduction states objectives explicitly, does NOT summarize results
- [ ] Main body organized by theme (not by paper), with TEC structure per subsection
- [ ] Conclusions are short and forward-looking, not an abstract repeat
- [ ] Language: English, accessible to non-specialists
- [ ] Cover letter explains why this review is timely for TrAC

### Elsevier-specific
- [ ] Submission via Editorial Manager (EVISE)
- [ ] Source files: Word (.docx) + separate figure files (TIFF/EPS/PDF)
- [ ] Reference manager compatible (EndNote, Zotero, Mendeley)
- [ ] CRediT author statement (optional but recommended)
- [ ] Declaration of competing interest

## 9. Key Differences from Other Review Journals

| Feature | TrAC | Chemical Reviews (ACS) | Environmental Science & Technology |
|---|---|---|---|
| Word count | ~4,000 | 15,000–30,000 | ~8,000 |
| References | ~50 | 200–500 | ~100 |
| Figures | ~4 | 10–20 | 6–8 |
| Style | Trend-focused, critical | Comprehensive, authoritative | Application-focused |
| Invitation | Mostly invited | Mostly invited | Open submission |
| **Core value** | **Identify and analyze trends** | **Comprehensive survey** | **Critical assessment** |

> **Implication for microplastics autofluorescence review**: With only 4000 words and 4 figures, every element must serve the core argument. The review should identify **trends in autofluorescence interference research**, not enumerate every study. The 5-part narrative structure (molecule → additive → environment → control points → image recognition) maps well onto 3–4 body subsections + 4 figures.

## 10. Recommended Figure Plan for Microplastics Autofluorescence Review

| Figure | Type | Content |
|---|---|---|
| **Figure 1** | Molecular mechanism diagram | Polymer molecular structures + functional groups + autofluorescence origin (π-π*, n-π* transitions) |
| **Figure 2** | Additive interference matrix | Heatmap or table-style diagram: additive type × fluorescence channel × interference intensity |
| **Figure 3** | Environmental aging pathway | Process flow: pristine plastic → weathering → fragmentation → spectral shift; with size-dependent fluorescence |
| **Figure 4** | Image recognition error propagation | Uncertainty chain: autofluorescence ambiguity → segmentation error → counting bias → quantification uncertainty |

**Graphical Abstract**: Simplified Figure 1 + Figure 4 combined, showing the core message: "molecular origin → image recognition distortion."
